# v11: concepts added from the MIT (6-3, MEng), Caltech (CS) and Stanford course review
## L2
pruning | Pruning & sparsity | ML systems | 1989 | PyTorch torch.nn.utils.prune, NVIDIA 2:4 structured sparsity (Ampere and later), SparseGPT, Wanda | Remove weights, neurons or attention heads that matter least, then fine-tune or distill to recover accuracy; structured patterns such as 2 of every 4 weights let sparse tensor cores skip the zeros.
chainrep | Chain replication | Distributed systems | 2004 | HDFS write pipeline, CRAQ, Azure Storage stream layer (chain-like), Hibari | Replicas form a chain: writes enter at the head and flow to the tail, reads are served by the tail, so every read sees only fully replicated writes; a master service repairs the chain when a node fails.
kbypass | Kernel-bypass networking | Performance | 2010 | DPDK, SPDK, AF_XDP, RDMA verbs, Seastar (ScyllaDB, Redpanda) | The application maps NIC queues into user space and polls them on dedicated cores, so packets skip syscalls, interrupts and the kernel TCP stack; latency drops to a few microseconds at the cost of a core spent spinning.
graphemb | Graph embeddings (DeepWalk, node2vec) | Machine learning | 2014 | PyTorch Geometric, DGL, PyTorch-BigGraph, Pinterest PinSage | Turn each node into a vector by running short random walks and training a skip-gram model so nodes that appear near each other in walks get similar vectors; the vectors feed recommendation, link prediction and identity matching.
## L3
itp | Proof assistants (Lean, Rocq/Coq, Isabelle) | Formal methods | 1972 | Lean 4 (mathlib), Rocq (formerly Coq), Isabelle/HOL, used for seL4, CompCert, AWS Cedar proofs | You state a theorem in a typed logic and build its proof step by step with tactics; a small trusted kernel checks every step, so a finished proof is machine-checked, not just reviewed.
